# From a familiar picture to precise understanding

Use when a report explains unfamiliar or complex concepts. Follow
[explanation design](explanation-design.md) for scope, descent and disclosure;
this module adds the connection from reader context to the actual subject.

## Start from what the reader already knows

Use relevant known work, examples, tools, vocabulary and learning preferences to
choose a familiar starting point. Let them change the explanation, not merely
add a greeting. Current instructions and the stated audience take precedence:
colleagues cannot be assumed to share the author's background. Do not invent a
profession, experience or preference, or require a profile interview, personal-data
lookup or saved learner profile. Use only context needed to teach the subject.

An experienced reader may already understand part of the system; spend depth
on the unfamiliar boundary. When context is absent, choose a broadly familiar
analogy or a plain explanation. Do not force an elementary metaphor onto everyone.

## Translate the analogy into the actual subject

A short analogy should explain the important relationship with little new
machinery and keep the early answer visible. Connect its useful elements to the
real concepts before relying on them, then let the actual terms carry the detail.
Keep the object recognizable as responsibilities, interactions and concrete rules
appear; introduce terms at their point of use.

State the analogy's limit where it could cause a wrong inference. It cannot
establish an unevidenced guarantee, ordering, permission or failure outcome.
Do not turn every metaphor element into a fictional component. If its exceptions
are harder to explain than the actual subject, shorten or replace the analogy.

The [request-tracking example](examples/explanation-depth.md) illustrates this
transition and possible detail views. Use [diagrams](diagrams.md) when choosing,
drawing or reviewing a figure. Apply the common [reading-depth checks](editorial-review.md#check-continuity-at-each-reading-depth)
to the finished explanation: layout, navigation and rendering cannot establish
understanding or measured learning gains.
